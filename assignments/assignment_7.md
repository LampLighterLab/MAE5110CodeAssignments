### Written report

A PDF "extended abstract" of your project, following the formatting guidelines from [Assignment 5](assignment_5.md) and [Assignment 6](assignment_6.md). You are allowed 3 pages for content, additional pages for bibliography.

Your manuscript should follow the following format:

- **Introduction** briefly set the context and motivation for your project (what is the gap?), and state your contribution.
- **Related Work** remember to focus on _fencing in_; you only need to point out related work that helps contextualize your contribution. Make the contrast clear!
- **Methods** Explain your implementation and methodology
- **Experiments** Explain how you are evaluating your implementation
- **Results & Discussion** show your actual results, and discuss how they should be interpreted
- **Conclusion & Outlook** briefly summarize what the reader take away, and what you would do next.

Note this is a lot to fit into 3 pages; it will require you to keep things focused on what is really important. The introduction and related work, in this case, should be especially concise.

As recommended in the Whitesides _How to write a paper_ paper, you should use assignment 6 as your draft, and iterate it as you implement and get results from those experiments. Early results will typically help you refine your contribution, and adjust the next set of experiments to sharpen your claims.

We will compare the experiments you've chosen to the ones you proposed in assignment 6; you are allowed/encouraged to change them, but the changes should be motivated.

### Video

Create a 2-3 minute video abstract of your work.
The video has the same intent and should follow the same overall structure as the written report, except you may omit related work (but not the motivation). You do _not_ need to have all experiments and all details in the video, but be very selective. You _do_ want to have more screentime alloted to results, especially hardware results (but also "cool" simulations, if some things didn't transfer). Showing side-by-side sim and hardware is ideal.

The recommended way to create the video is with a screen-recording (Zoom actually works well for this) of a presentation. If you don't want to record your own voice-over (which can require practice), you may include it as a caption, and use AI to narrate.
